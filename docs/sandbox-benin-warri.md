# Benin–Warri c. 1610: sources

The `benin-warri` template (`lib/sandbox/benin-warri.mjs`) is a **simulation inspired by history; its inhabitants are fictional.** This page lists every historical fact the template and its History panel use, with a real, checkable source for each. It was written before the template's content.

**Rules this page follows:**

- An uncertain date is marked "c.".
- No fact, date or source is invented. A detail that could not be sourced is left out and listed under **Open questions**.
- **Oral tradition** is marked as such. A source written **after 1610** is marked as later.
- Real historical people (rulers, Dom Domingos, Maria Pereira) appear **only** in the viewer's History panel, quoting the sourced facts. No invented words or deeds are given to them. Both royal houses, the Oba of Benin's and the Olu of Warri's, still reign today.
- The Portuguese trade in this region also involved enslaved people. The simulation never models buying, selling or owning people.

## How the sources were checked

This was compiled from a build environment without access to the books or journal archives themselves. Each entry says how it was checked:

- **record**: the bibliographic record (title, author, year, publisher or journal) was confirmed in a library or publisher catalogue;
- **abstract**: the publisher's abstract or a review was read;
- **cited**: the claim was found in a secondary work that cites this source (named), not read in the source itself;
- **secondary**: the claim rests on the named secondary source itself (for example an encyclopaedia article), which is weaker.
- **search**: the claim was read in a web-search summary of the named page; the page itself could not be opened from the build environment. Weaker than **record**: it should be checked on the page.

A historian should check every **cited** and **secondary** entry against the original pages before this world is presented as accurate (see **Reviewed by** at the end).

## The hard truth

Portuguese priests came to this region on trading ships, and were told to support themselves by trading in enslaved people (W13). The trade with Benin and with Warri involved enslaved people as well as ivory, cloth, pepper, coral beads and brass manillas (B6; Fonseca 2016 has slavery, "escravatura", in its title). **The simulation never models buying, selling or owning people.** The History panel says this plainly.

## Decisions taken from the sources

- **No church, monastery or resident priest in 1610.** The sources found (W8, W9) say there was no resident priest in 1597, and that a 1620 report still found Christians only at the capital. No source found shows a church or a resident priest by 1610. The mission church in the first plan for this template was **removed**, and a test fails if a church or a resident priest is added without a source.
- **The Augustinians** appear in the History panel as visitors of the 1570s (W7). The world, set c. 1610, has none.
- **Benin and Warri in 1610.** Warri was founded from Benin (W1), and Benin is usually held to have been senior in Warri's early years. How dependent Warri still was in 1610 is not settled by the sources found. Ayida (cited in Wikipedia "Atorongboye") reads the Olu's baptism as a way of strengthening Warri's position in relation to Benin. The world models two separate kingdoms at peace that trade with each other, and the History panel records that the sources found do not settle the question.
- **Peace.** The simulation's standoffs and wars are switched off for this template (`peaceful: true` on its life rule). No source was found for a conflict to model, and the template must not invent one between real peoples.
- **Technology.** Research starts at the period's levels, and future technology is off by default (`progress: false` on the life rule). A per-world rule can turn progression on.
- **Cassava** is left out. The sources found are weak or conflicting: a continental study says cassava reached Africa's coasts before 1600, an FAO summary says it came to the Warri area from Fernando Po but was not widespread until about 1800, and another source says the late 18th century. Farming in the world yields "farm produce", without naming a crop.
- **The two "silver crowns".** Palace tradition says Domingos brought two crowns from the king; the art historian Kathy Curnow found that one is gilt silver and the other bronze or brass, and treats their origin as tradition. **Not used.** It stays an open question.

## The fictional storyline

**Fictional, clearly labelled, inspired by history (W10, W11):** a prince of Warri, with an invented name, has been studying in Lisbon. Early in the world he comes home on a Portuguese ship with his Portuguese wife, also invented. Their arrival is an event marked "Storyline (fictional, inspired by history)". Neither says or does anything attributed to a real person, and neither is Dom Domingos or Maria Pereira.

## Open questions (not in the world)

1. A first meeting with the Portuguese **at Ijala**: no source found.
1a. **The layout of Benin's walls in the world** is illustrative, not a map: the number of village enclosures (six), where they lie, their size, and where each of the nine gates is on the ring are not from a source, and the world is smaller than life (the city's inner iya, 12–15 km round, is a ring 900 m across). The gates' names (Wikipedia "Ancient gates of Benin Kingdom", secondary) are not given to particular gates in the world.
1b. **Who built which wall when**: traditions credit Oba Oguola (c. 1280) and Oba Ewuare (c. 1440) with parts of the city's earthworks (Wikipedia "Benin Moat", secondary, from tradition). In the History panel as tradition only.
2. An earlier settlement **near the mouth of the Forcados River**: only the tradition in W2 (the route by the Forcados to Ijala) was found. It is in the History panel as tradition, not built.
3. Ode-Itsekiri's **chiefs' quarters** and its **market**: no source found for 1610. Not built. (Warri's people eat at home in the world.)
4. Warri's **council of chiefs** in 1610: no source found for this period. Lloyd 1963 describes a highly centralised government around 1800. The world gives the Olu chiefs at the palace (a job), without naming offices.
5. **Canoe building** as a craft at Warri c. 1610: no source found. Canoes are used; their building is not modelled.
6. **Iron working** at Benin c. 1610: no source found in this pass (likely in Bradbury 1957). Not in the world's starting crafts.
7. The **rainforest** round the capital: no source found in this pass. The land round the capital is drawn as forest; this is scenery, not a claim. (**Benin's markets** are now sourced, from a later account: M1.)
8. The **Benin–Warri relationship** in 1610 (above).
9. **Cassava** (above). 10. The **silver crowns** (above).
12. **Animals outside Benin** (at Warri, the Urhobo and the Ijaw) c. 1610: no source found in this pass. Not in the world.
11. **Personal names**: the inhabitants' names are common given names in Edo, Itsekiri, Urhobo, Ịjọ and Portuguese, chosen for the simulation. They should be reviewed by speakers of each language. None is the name of a ruler of either royal house.
